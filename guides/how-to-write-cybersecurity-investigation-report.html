SECURITY REPORTING GUIDE

How to Write a Beginner Cybersecurity Investigation Report

A useful investigation report lets another person understand what was reviewed, what the evidence shows, and what remains uncertain. Clear reporting is more important than dramatic conclusions.

1. State the scope

Identify the system or fictional scenario, data sources, time range, authorization, and questions being investigated. Note what was not reviewed.

2. Preserve and describe evidence

List the log files, event IDs, packet captures, or supplied records examined. Include timestamps and relevant identifiers. Keep original evidence unchanged where possible and record how any working copy was created.

3. Build a timeline

Order related events by time and normalize time zones when needed. Include the event, source, account or asset, and supporting record. Do not combine unrelated events merely because they occurred close together.

4. Separate observations from interpretation

Observation: the log contains three failed logons from a recorded source address. Interpretation: the pattern may indicate password guessing, but could also have an approved or benign explanation. State what additional evidence would confirm or weaken the hypothesis.

5. Explain impact and next steps

Describe the affected account or system only as far as evidence supports. Recommend proportionate next steps such as validating ownership, checking related authentication events, preserving logs, or escalating through the approved incident process. Do not claim compromise without sufficient evidence.

Simple report outline

# Investigation Report
## Summary
## Scope and Authorization
## Evidence Reviewed
## Timeline
## Findings
## Impact and Uncertainty
## Recommended Next Steps
## Limitations
